# Accessibility Review — Fit my Ride UX Spine

## Overall verdict

The spine takes colorblind-safety seriously and gets the structural bet right: a blue↔orange (not red/green) heat scale with a lightness peak at the center, tripled up with a glyph and a printed number, plus an explicit "never color alone" rule for errors and unknown cells. Most stated dark-theme text contrasts check out by calculation (body text, muted text, neon, the diagram card). The two things that actually need fixing before build: the focus ring's claimed universal visibility against "every fill" doesn't hold up against 4 of the 5 heat-tier colors by the numbers, and the Geometry Diagram — the one place that visually explains what each of the 12 abbreviations physically measures — has no accessible text alternative for screen-reader users. Several other findings are smaller gaps or unverified claims rather than outright failures.

### Findings

- **high** The focus ring is not visible against most heat-tier fills, contradicting EXPERIENCE.md's own claim. `focus-ring` (`#82E2FF`) computed against each heat fill (WCAG non-text 3:1 minimum): vs `heat-ideal` (`#F1EEF7`) ≈ **1.28:1**, vs `heat-slight-long` (`#FFBF80`) ≈ **1.10:1**, vs `heat-slight-short` (`#8FB8FF`) ≈ **1.36:1**, vs `heat-too-long` (`#F07028`) ≈ **2.02:1** — all fail. Only vs `heat-too-short` (`#3D6FE0`) ≈ **3.14:1**, a razor-thin pass. This directly contradicts EXPERIENCE.md §Accessibility Floor: "The focus ring uses `{colors.focus-ring}` and stays visible against every fill." (EXPERIENCE.md:121; token values DESIGN.md:17, 23-32). The mock (`.working/key-results-desktop.html:148`, `outline-offset:1px`) avoids the failure only because its table-cell padding happens to place the ring over the dark `surface` background instead of the fill — but DESIGN.md's own dense-grid tokens (`spacing.cell-gap: 2px`, DESIGN.md:79, "cells are 64×40 with a 2px gap so the grid reads as a heatmap", DESIGN.md:176) leave a 2px cell gap with no documented padding inside the `heat-cell` component (DESIGN.md:109-113 lists only radius/typography/min-width/height, no padding). A 2px ring with 1px offset needs ~3px of clearance it may not reliably have in the real (non-table-mock) implementation, especially between two adjacent colored cells with no unknown/dashed cell between them. *Fix:* pick a focus-ring treatment proven against all 5 fills — e.g. a ring color with guaranteed 3:1 against the lightest (`heat-ideal`) and most saturated (`heat-too-long`) fills, or a technique that doesn't depend on gap size (double-ring/halo, or an inset ring drawn in each cell's own contrasting foreground color). Verify against the real grid layout, not the padded table mock.
- **high** The Geometry Diagram has no accessible text alternative despite being informational content. The inlined SVG (`.working/key-results-desktop.html:193-335`) has no `<title>`, `<desc>`, `role="img"`, or `aria-label`; the only text near it is a visible caption describing authorship/provenance ("Original artwork, drawn to match the vendor sheets' own layout and labels…", line 337), not what the diagram shows. Per EXPERIENCE.md itself, the diagram's actual job is to answer "what does TT actually measure on the bike" (EXPERIENCE.md:86, :165) — a purely visual, spatial answer (which line on the frame is which parameter) that a screen-reader user gets zero equivalent of. Column headers do carry each parameter's full name as text (mitigating the abbreviation-decoding half of the problem, DESIGN.md:192), but not the diagram's spatial "this line here is TT" explanation. This is a WCAG 1.1.1 (Level A) gap, not just a nice-to-have. *Fix:* add a concise `aria-label`/`<title>` naming it as a reference schematic, and either a short structured text description (e.g. a definition list: "TT — Top Tube Length: horizontal top-tube span") or mark the SVG `role="presentation"` if the column headers' full names are deemed a sufficient substitute — but that decision should be explicit in the spine, not left unstated.
- **medium** The colorblind-simulation claim is asserted, not evidenced. DESIGN.md states "Contrast was checked, not eyeballed... adjacent tiers stay clearly separable (ΔE ≥ 27) under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia" (DESIGN.md:156) and .memlog.md repeats the same ΔE≥27 figure (.memlog.md:19), but no simulation output, tool name, script, or CVD-rendered preview exists anywhere in the workspace (checked `.working/` — only plain-rendered `key-results-desktop-preview.png` and `key-fit-form-preview.png`, no simulated variant). The underlying design choice is structurally sound as a backup even without the evidence: the five tiers monotonically decrease/increase in lightness moving outward from `heat-ideal` (relative luminance ≈0.87 → 0.60/0.47 → 0.30/0.18), which stays separable under CVD regardless of hue perception — so this is a documentation/evidence gap rather than a demonstrated safety failure. *Fix:* before sign-off, actually run the five hex values through a CVD simulator (e.g. Coblis, or a programmatic Brettel/Viénot simulation) and keep the output (numbers or an image) alongside the spine, so the "ΔE ≥ 27" figure is a checked fact instead of a remembered one.
- **medium** `cell-unknown-border` fails non-text contrast against its own background. `#4A4760` against `surface` (`#1E1D27`, the table's background — `.table-wrap{background:var(--surface)}`, mock line 101) computes to ≈**1.87:1**, well under the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 minimum for a meaningful UI boundary (DESIGN.md:34, 117). The unknown state is still legible because the "—" glyph itself is in `text-muted` (≈7.1:1 against surface, safe) — but the dashed border meant to make the cell read as a deliberate "unknown" box rather than a gap in the grid is hard to perceive, particularly at a glance across a dense table. *Fix:* darken/lighten the border token to clear 3:1 against `surface`, or accept that the border is cosmetic and let the "—" + accessible name carry the meaning (and say so explicitly, rather than implying the outline itself is load-bearing).
- **medium** The heat-cell glyph pairs rely on filled-vs-hollow variants of the same triangle at small size, which is itself a hard-to-perceive distinction for low-vision (not just colorblind) users. Glyphs are `▼` too-short / `▽` slightly-short and `▲` too-long / `△` slightly-long (DESIGN.md:190), rendered at 10px (`.cell .g{font-size:10px}`, mock line 138) next to a 13px value. The fill/outline contrast between e.g. `▼` and `▽` is subtle at that size — precisely the kind of low-acuity confusability the color+glyph redundancy was meant to route around. *Fix:* either bump glyph size, or swap the "slightly" pair for shapes with a clearer silhouette difference from their "too" counterpart (e.g. a half-filled or differently-proportioned mark) rather than fill-state alone.
- **medium** The show-excluded toggle's row-marking mechanism for excluded bikes isn't specified as non-color. EXPERIENCE.md says only "On, excluded bikes appear at the bottom, visibly marked" (EXPERIENCE.md:69) and the State Patterns table doesn't describe the marking (EXPERIENCE.md:88-103). If "visibly marked" ends up meaning a color/opacity treatment alone (e.g. dimmed rows), that's a color-only-meaning gap inconsistent with the rest of the spine's own discipline. *Fix:* specify the marker explicitly — a text badge ("Excluded — [reason]") or icon, matching the rigor already applied to the unknown-cell and error states.
- **medium** Native-table semantics and arrow-key cell navigation are both promised without reconciling them. EXPERIENCE.md says "The table is a real table: row and column headers" (EXPERIENCE.md:119) and separately "Arrow keys move between cells. Enter on a cell opens its popover" (EXPERIENCE.md:107) — a plain HTML `<table>` doesn't give cells arrow-key-navigable focus on its own; that behavior normally requires the ARIA `grid`/`treegrid` pattern (roving `tabindex`, explicit `role`s) layered on top. Building this naively risks losing either the native semantics or the described keyboard behavior for screen-reader users specifically (mouse/sighted-keyboard users would likely still "work" either way). *Fix:* have the spine (or the handoff to architecture/build) name the ARIA grid pattern explicitly as the implementation target, since it's the piece that reconciles both stated requirements.
- **low** `heat-too-short-text` (`#FFFFFF` on `#3D6FE0`) computes to ≈**4.62:1** — passes the stated "at least 4.5:1" bar (DESIGN.md:156) but by under 3% headroom, the thinnest margin of the five tiers. *Fix:* re-check against the actual rendered/anti-aliased colors once built rather than treating this pairing as settled; a small headroom buffer (e.g. darken the fill slightly) would be cheap insurance.
- **low** The documented cell accessible-name pattern omits the "distance from range" figure the visual popover shows. EXPERIENCE.md's example is "Reach, 39.4 centimeters, slightly long, your range 38.5 to 40.0" (EXPERIENCE.md:119) — no distance-from-range — while the popover mock shows an extra line, "0.4 cm above range" (mock line 382). Sighted users get one more data point than the accessible name currently promises. *Fix:* fold the distance into the accessible-name pattern, or confirm it's intentionally left out.
- **low** No accessible-name pattern is given for unknown/not-evaluated cells, only the general promise that they're "announced in words, never as an empty cell" (EXPERIENCE.md:120). The valued-cell case gets a worked example; the unknown case doesn't. *Fix:* add one, e.g. "Reach, unknown, no data for this bike" / "Fork Length, not evaluated, no evaluation method for this parameter yet" — reusing the popover copy already specified in State Patterns (EXPERIENCE.md:98-99).

## What's already handled well

- The heat scale's core redundancy is real and consistent: every colored cell carries hue + a distinct glyph + the printed number (DESIGN.md:190, EXPERIENCE.md:81-82), and neon/primary are explicitly fenced off from ever carrying fit meaning (DESIGN.md:151-152, 168) — the red/green trap is explicitly named and avoided (DESIGN.md:168, EXPERIENCE.md:153).
- Unknown vs. not-evaluated is handled honestly and consistently: dashed outline + muted "—" + explicit header copy ("not evaluated") + distinct popover text for each reason, never a blank cell (DESIGN.md:166, 191; EXPERIENCE.md:98-99) — and the "—" text itself is safely contrasted (≈7:1) even though the border isn't (see findings).
- Body text, muted text, neon and the diagram card's own internal contrast are all comfortably above AA by calculation: text/background ≈15.5:1, text-muted/background ≈7.1:1, neon/background ≈14.5:1, and the diagram's dark line art (`#424242`) and black labels on `diagram-card` (`#EDEAF3`) compute to ≈8.5:1 and ≈17.7:1 respectively — the one deliberate light-theme exception is well-executed and doesn't leak into the rest of the dark UI (DESIGN.md:212 explicitly forbids that).
- Sort state isn't color-only: the active column gets both a neon color change and a direction arrow glyph (EXPERIENCE.md:66, mock line 346).
- Validation errors are explicitly specified as color + text, never color alone (EXPERIENCE.md:94), with corrective, specific copy ("Enter a height between 120 and 230 cm.") rather than generic messages (EXPERIENCE.md:52).
- Keyboard parity for the popover is explicit — focus triggers the same popover as hover/tap, and hover-only affordances are explicitly banned (EXPERIENCE.md:67, 109, 111) — this is exactly the kind of thing that's easy to miss and wasn't.
- Reduced motion is explicitly respected (EXPERIENCE.md:123), and `aria-sort` plus a concrete cell accessible-name pattern are both named (EXPERIENCE.md:119) rather than left to "the table should be accessible" hand-waving.
