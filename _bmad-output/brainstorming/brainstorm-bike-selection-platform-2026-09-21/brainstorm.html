Fitting a rider to a bike is a measurement problem wearing a search‑engine costume.

A Creative Partner session between a rider (user) and a coach, working out a bike‑selection platform: prompt‑driven search over an auto‑filled geometry database, fit filters, and a map of where to see / test / buy.

Mode: Creative Partner Techniques: Morphological → SCAMPER → What‑If ● complete Updated 2026‑09‑22 21:37

The whole session in one picture: a rider is a set of measurements (height, inseam, torso, reach of the arms…). A bike is a set of measurements (stack, reach, top tube, seat angle…). The platform's job is to compare the two sets honestly — tier by tier, source by source, vendor by vendor — and say how well they line up, not just whether they do.

Final synthesis

Four spokes, one wheel

Everything the session touched converges into four reusable patterns — the true payoff of trading ideas for a day and a half.

v1
FIT ENGINE
1

The canonical‑mapping pattern

Name‑synonym map, unit normalization (→cm), and usage‑taxonomy mapping are one reusable pattern: a canonical target plus a per‑vendor dictionary — the same trick, applied three times.

2

The trust‑envelope pattern

Provenance + date per field is one trust envelope that quietly powers three separate features: vendor‑conflict arbitration, the "verified by brand" badge, and the estimated‑vs‑confirmed measurement label.

3

The input‑precision spectrum

Height‑only mode and geometry‑target/expert mode aren't competing entry points — they're two ends of one spectrum, from "just my height" to "I know my exact reach in mm".

4

Deferring shops sharpens the bet

Pushing the whole shop/map layer out of Must resolves the "map‑not‑essential‑to‑fit" tension outright: the fit layer alone must be worth using before any where‑to‑buy utility exists.
